Our story · Our journey across India

From one forest estate to 700+ acres

We didn’t set out to build a food brand. It began with one question: what if people, food and nature could thrive together? Every pin is a collective. Scroll, and the journey travels from the Western Ghats across the Deccan to Central India, and on to Mumbai.

The journey, collective by collective

2019 · Poomaale 1.0, Coorg · Western Ghats

We expected neglect. We found resilience.

In 2019, our first question led to Poomaale in Coorg, the first Wilderness Collective (today we call it Poomaale 1.0). The coffee estate had been largely unattended for 7–8 years. Its coffee trees, already 30–40 years old, were growing with very little human intervention, while parts of the estate had slowly begun returning to forest.

Coffee plants were still finding their way through the forest. Streams continued to flow. Birds, insects and wildlife had slowly reclaimed the land.

  • 128acres of coffee, forest and streams
  • 66member families
  • 30–40year-old coffee trees
  • 2 → 6bags of coffee per acre, no chemicals

“Nature doesn’t stop trying. Sometimes, it simply waits for us to stop getting in its way.”

That experience stayed with us, not because it gave us coffee, but because it changed the questions we started asking.
The questions we started asking

What if farming didn’t have to compete with forests?

  1. 01What if farming didn’t have to compete with forests?
  2. 02What if regenerating a landscape could become the starting point for growing food?
  3. 03What if the quality of an ingredient was simply a reflection of the health of the ecosystem that produced it?

Those questions became Bewild, and from Coorg we bring coffee, cardamom and honey from forest-integrated landscapes.

    Poomaale 2.0 · Kakkabe, Coorg · 1,040 m

    Next door, and three years wiser

    Across a short road from Poomaale 1.0 lies a second Coorg collective: 100 acres of rainforest over an old coffee and cardamom plantation that had seen little human activity for a decade. Two hillocks, two perennial streams and countless natural springs run through it.

    Everything three years of Poomaale 1.0 taught us went into it. About half the land stays a low-intervention forest, food is grown only on the other half, and building is limited to 3% of the land. Over 40 spices, medicinal herbs and superfoods are planned, from pepper vines to oranges, figs, pomelos and avocados.

    • 100acres of rainforest
    • 33member families
    • 40+spices, herbs & superfoods planned
    Hammiyala · near Madapura, Coorg · seed community forming

    A coffee estate, becoming a food forest

    About 45 minutes from Madikeri, in the heart of Coorg, a former irrigated coffee plantation is being reimagined as a food forest: a wilderness collective, revisited. Two hillocks, a perennial stream and natural springs shape the land.

    At least half of it will stay a low-intervention forest. The rest is planned for medicinal herbs, spices, fruit trees and pepper vines, with forest-integrated homes for each family. Its seed community of early members is forming now.

    • 115acres
    • ½kept as low-intervention forest
    • 3collectives now in Coorg
    2020 · Hyderabad Collective · Deccan Plateau

    A dry landscape, brought back to life

    Site work on the Hyderabad Collective began in 2020. Over the next two years the land was given water and shade again: solar became its main source of power by May 2021, and 54 check dams were built between December 2021 and October 2022.

    From the Hyderabad Collective, we bring rice varieties, rosella and spices such as mustard, turmeric and chilli.

    • 132acres
    • 88families
    • 54check dams
      Ratapani, near Bhopal · Central India

      Heirloom wheat from the heartland

      In Central India, a stone’s throw from the Ratapani Tiger Reserve and less than 55 km from Bhopal, a 122-acre landscape is our next wilderness to restore.

      From Bhopal, we bring wheat varieties including Sharbati and Kapli: freshly milled, from the agricultural heartland.

      • 122acres
      • <55 kmfrom Bhopal
      • 2heirloom wheats: Sharbati & Kapli

        Today

        700+ acres under regeneration

        What began with regenerating one abandoned coffee landscape has grown into a network of collectives across very different ecologies, producing coffee, rice, wheat, honey, spices, pulses and other forest-friendly produce.

        Our ingredients now find their way into homes, cafés, restaurants and stores across India. Yet we still think of ourselves as students. Every landscape teaches us. Every harvest asks better questions than the one before.

        The soil comes first, the produce follows.

        How we think about impact

        The Four Returns

        A landscape’s success isn’t measured by yield alone. Four principles shape every collective.

        More life in the landscape.

        We track soil biology, biodiversity and water systems to understand whether the land is becoming healthier. At Poomaale:

        • 17×fungal biomass
        • 60+bird species
        • 125butterfly species
        • 60+fungi recorded

        2 → 6 bags of coffee per acre, without chemical inputs

        Better livelihoods connected to healthy landscapes.

        Our collectives bring together growers, ecologists, researchers, local communities, food experts and knowledge keepers, creating livelihoods while preserving the knowledge that helps each landscape thrive.

        • Growers
        • Ecologists
        • Researchers
        • Local communities
        • Food experts
        • Knowledge keepers

        Regeneration that can sustain itself.

        A landscape needs to remain productive and economically viable for regeneration to last. We look at how ecological health, production and livelihoods can strengthen one another over time.

        • Ecological health
        • Production
        • Livelihoods

        Stronger communities and shared knowledge.

        Regeneration brings people together to participate, share knowledge, collaborate and build relationships around the landscapes they care for.
